Overview

English grammar topics for every learner

1. Parts of speech

Learn nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, articles, and interjections.

2. Sentence building

Understand simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex sentences with accurate structure.

3. Tenses and verbs

Master all major tense patterns and verb forms for past, present, and future communication.

4. Articles and determiners

Foundation

Parts of speech

Nouns

Nouns name people, places, things, feelings, or ideas.

  • Proper noun: Emma
  • Common noun: book
  • Collective noun: team
  • Abstract noun: happiness
Example: The teacher gave the students a lesson.

Pronouns

Pronouns replace nouns to avoid repetition.

  • Personal: I, you, he, she, it
  • Possessive: mine, yours, his
  • Reflexive: myself, himself
  • Relative: who, which, that
Example: She is my sister. She works in Dubai.

Verbs

Verbs show action, state, or occurrence.

  • Action verb: run
  • Linking verb: is
  • Helping verb: have
  • Modal verb: can
Example: They are studying English.

Adjectives

Adjectives describe nouns and pronouns.

  • Descriptive: beautiful, tall
  • Quantitative: many, few
  • Demonstrative: this, those
  • Possessive: my, our
Example: This is a beautiful garden.

Adverbs

Adverbs describe verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs.

  • Manner: slowly, carefully
  • Time: yesterday, later
  • Place: here, outside
  • Degree: very, too
Example: She speaks very clearly.

Prepositions

Prepositions show relationship in time, place, direction, and manner.

  • Time: at, on, in
  • Place: under, beside, between
  • Direction: into, onto, towards
Example: The keys are under the table.

Conjunctions

Conjunctions connect words, phrases, or clauses.

  • Coordinating: and, but, or
  • Subordinating: because, although, while
  • Correlative: both...and
Example: I stayed home because it was raining.

Articles

Articles show whether a noun is specific or general.

  • A / an: singular, non-specific
  • The: specific noun
  • Zero article: no article needed
Example: I saw a dog. The dog was black.

Structure

Sentence types and sentence structure

Simple sentence

One independent clause.

Example: I read books.

Compound sentence

Two or more independent clauses connected by coordinating conjunctions.

Example: I finished work, and I went home.

Complex sentence

One independent clause and one dependent clause.

Example: Although it was late, we continued studying.

Compound-complex sentence

At least two independent clauses and one dependent clause.

Example: Although he was tired, he worked, and he finished the task.

Declarative

Statement.

Example: She likes coffee.

Interrogative

Question.

Example: Where are you going?

Imperative

Command or request.

Example: Open the door.

Exclamatory

Strong emotion.

Example: What a beautiful day!

Sentence parts

Subject, verb, object, complement, predicate.

Example: My brother plays football.

Verb system

Tenses and verb forms

Present simple

Used for habits, routines, facts, and general truths.

Example: She walks to school every day.

Present continuous

Used for actions happening now or around now.

Example: They are watching a movie right now.

Present perfect

Used for actions that happened before now with a connection to the present.

Example: I have finished my homework.

Present perfect continuous

Used for actions that started in the past and are still continuing.

Example: He has been working here for three years.

Past simple

Used for completed actions in the past.

Example: We visited our grandparents last weekend.

Past continuous

Used for past actions in progress at a specific time.

Example: She was cooking when I arrived.

Past perfect

Used for an action completed before another past action.

Example: He had left before the meeting started.

Past perfect continuous

Used for actions continuing before another past action.

Example: They had been waiting for an hour before the train came.

Future simple

Used for predictions, promises, and planned decisions.

Example: We will travel next month.

Future continuous

Used for an action that will be in progress at a future time.

Example: This time tomorrow, I will be flying to London.

Future perfect

Used for actions that will be completed before a future time.

Example: By 8 PM, I will have finished the report.

Future perfect continuous

Used for actions continuing up to a future time.

Example: By next week, she will have been working here for a year.

Important topics

More grammar topics every learner should know

Articles and determiners

  • a, an, the
  • some, any
  • many, much
  • few, little
  • each, every, either, neither

Subject-verb agreement

Example: She writes well. They write well.

Active and passive voice

Active: They cleaned the room.
Passive: The room was cleaned.

Conditionals

  • Zero conditional
  • First conditional
  • Second conditional
  • Third conditional
  • Mixed conditionals

Modals

  • can / could
  • may / might
  • must / have to
  • should / ought to
  • will / would

Reported speech

Direct: He said, “I am tired.”
Indirect: He said that he was tired.

Infinitives and gerunds

I enjoy reading.
I want to read.

Relative clauses

Example: The woman who called me is my teacher.

Comparatives and superlatives

Example: She is taller than me. This is the tallest building.

Advanced grammar

Advanced English grammar topics

Subjunctive mood

Used to express demands, wishes, and suggestions.

Example: I suggest that he leave now.

Inversion

Used in questions and formal style.

Example: Never have I seen such a beautiful place.

Ellipsis

Leaving out repeated words for brevity.

Example: I like coffee, and she does too.

Nominalization

Turning verbs or adjectives into nouns.

Example: decide → decision

Reduced clauses

Shorter versions of clauses used in writing and speech.

Example: Because she was tired, she slept early. → Being tired, she slept early.

Apposition

Adding a noun phrase to explain another noun.

Example: My brother, a doctor, works in London.

Punctuation rules

  • Full stop at end of statement
  • Comma between clauses
  • Colon before a list
  • Semi-colon for related ideas
  • Apostrophe for possession

Formal vs informal English

Use appropriate grammar for different situations.

Formal: I would appreciate your response.
Informal: Thanks! Let me know.

Common mistakes

Frequent errors in English grammar

1. Subject-verb disagreement

Wrong: She go to school.
Correct: She goes to school.

2. Double negatives

Wrong: I don't know nothing.
Correct: I don't know anything.

3. Sentence fragments

Wrong: Because I was tired.
Correct: Because I was tired, I slept early.

4. Misplaced modifiers

Wrong: I almost drove 200 kilometers.
Correct: I drove almost 200 kilometers.

5. Wrong article use

Wrong: I went to a hospital. (if speaking of a specific hospital)
Correct: I went to the hospital.

6. Incorrect tense shifts

Wrong: When I arrived, he is cooking.
Correct: When I arrived, he was cooking.
